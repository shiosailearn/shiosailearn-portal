Stellar · South Africa

A research tool built for the languages you actually work in.

Stellar is a free, offline-first desktop environment for humanities scholars. No cloud. No AI. No subscription. South African language support is in progress — and we need people who know the languages to help get it right.

Why South Africa

One of the world's most linguistically complex research contexts.

South Africa has 12 official languages, a long tradition of humanities and archival scholarship, and a research community that operates across multiple language contexts simultaneously. Within the Global South, it stands apart — not just for linguistic diversity, but for the depth of its academic infrastructure.

Most research tools are built for English. A few support one or two European languages. Stellar is designed to go further — and South Africa is a meaningful place to start.

01

12 official languages, including isiZulu, isiXhosa, Sesotho, and Afrikaans — each with its own academic and archival tradition.

02

Strong humanities research culture across political science, history, linguistics, and social science.

03

A growing digital humanities community with existing NLP resources and language infrastructure to build on.

The Tool

What Stellar does.

Stellar is a single desktop app for humanities researchers. Everything in one place, on your own machine.

Now Citation manager — store, tag, and search papers and books.
Now Markdown editor with bidirectional WikiLinks — connect ideas as you write.
Now Qualitative coding tool — code text data, build themes, without expensive commercial software.
Now Citation network graph — visualize how your sources connect.
Planned NLP-assisted qualitative analysis — using language resources to process multilingual research material.

That last item is where South African language resources matter most. If isiZulu or isiXhosa NLP data can be integrated into Stellar's analysis layer, the tool stops being a translated English app and starts being something that actually understands the material researchers work with. That's useful not just in South Africa, but across the African continent — for any researcher working in languages that current tools ignore.

Localization here isn't cosmetic. It's infrastructure.

Get Involved

We're looking for terminology reviewers.

Stellar already supports Japanese, English, French, and Afrikaans. The next step is South African languages — starting with these five:

isiZulu isiXhosa Sepedi Setswana Sesotho

We're not asking for full translation. We need people who can review a small set of academic and research UI terms — around 100 to 200 words — and flag anything that doesn't sit right. Words like citation, annotation, qualitative coding, archival source.

All contributors are credited in the project. No prior software experience needed.

If you work with South African languages in an academic context — or know someone who does — we'd like to hear from you.
